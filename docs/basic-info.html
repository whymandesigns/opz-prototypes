Basic Info

The Basic Info section is the foundation of every Initiative. It establishes the initiative's identity — who owns it, what it's trying to achieve, and the timeframe it operates within.

The Basic Info form when creating a new Initiative. Required fields are marked — the initiative can't be saved without a Name and Owner.

Fields

Field Required Description
name Required A short, descriptive title for the initiative. Shown in lists, dashboards, and notifications. Aim for clarity over cleverness — "Q3 EMEA Expansion" beats "Project Falcon".
description Optional A rich-text field for context, background, and scope. Use it to explain why this initiative exists, what success looks like, and what's explicitly out of scope. Supports bold, bullet lists, and links.
owner Required The single accountable person for this initiative. The owner receives all escalations, status reminders, and is the default contact shown to stakeholders. Only one owner is allowed — use contributors for additional team members.
status Optional Current health of the initiative. Defaults to Draft until manually updated.
Draft On Track At Risk Off Track Complete
start_date Optional When work begins. Used to calculate timeline position on roadmap views and to trigger kickoff reminders for the owner.
end_date Optional Target completion date. When an initiative passes its end date without being marked Complete, it is automatically flagged as overdue and the owner is notified.

Ownership vs. Contributors

An Initiative has exactly one Owner — the single accountable person. This keeps escalation paths clear and avoids diffusion of responsibility.

Additional team members working on the initiative should be added as Contributors in the Project section. Contributors can edit the initiative but don't receive ownership-level notifications.

Best practice The Owner should be the person who will present this initiative in your ROB meeting — not necessarily the person doing the most work. Align owner with accountability, not workload.

Status lifecycle

Status is a manual signal — it reflects the owner's judgment about initiative health, not an automated rollup. Update it weekly, ideally before your ROB meeting.

1
Draft
Initiative is being scoped. Not yet visible to stakeholders outside the team. Use this phase to fill in details and get alignment on the plan.
2
On Track / At Risk / Off Track
Initiative is active. Owner updates status weekly. "At Risk" signals a potential problem; "Off Track" signals the plan needs to change.
3
Complete
Work is done. Initiative moves to read-only mode. OKR progress is snapshotted and a completion summary is sent to all contributors.

Naming tips

Initiative names appear in ROB meeting agendas, Slack notifications, and executive dashboards. A good name is:

  • Specific — "EMEA Localization Launch" not "Localization"
  • Time-bounded — include the quarter or year when relevant
  • Outcome-oriented — name the result, not the activity
Avoid codenames Codenames (Project Falcon, Operation Sunrise) look cool but confuse stakeholders who weren't in the room when the name was chosen. Use descriptive names from day one.
